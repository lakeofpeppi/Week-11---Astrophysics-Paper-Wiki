Dark energy

The unknown thing that makes the universe's expansion speed up.

Plain explanation

Since 1998 we've known the universe isn't just expanding, its expansion is accelerating. Whatever causes this is called dark energy, and it makes up about 70% of the universe's energy.

The simplest explanation is a constant energy of empty space (the cosmological constant, Λ), which has an equation-of-state parameter w = −1. The standard cosmological model with it is called ΛCDM.

Analogy: Imagine throwing a ball upward and, instead of slowing down, it speeds up. Something unseen must be pushing.

Why astronomers care

  • It's one of the biggest unsolved problems in physics.
  • Small deviations from w = −1, or any clumping, would mean new physics beyond ΛCDM.
