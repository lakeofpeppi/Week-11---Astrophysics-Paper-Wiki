Free-floating planet

A planet-mass object drifting through space without orbiting any star.

Plain explanation

Some are planets that were thrown out of their home systems. Others may have formed alone, like a very small failed star.

With no star nearby they're faint, but also easier to study, because there's no bright star drowning out their light.

Analogy: A runaway who left home, or someone who was never part of a household at all.

Why astronomers care

  • They tell us how often planetary systems eject planets.
  • Isolated objects make clean benchmarks for giant-planet atmospheres.
