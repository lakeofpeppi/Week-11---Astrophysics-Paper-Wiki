Debris disc

A flat ring of dust and gas made from broken-up rocky bodies orbiting a star.

Plain explanation

When asteroids or small planets break apart, their pieces spread out into a flat ring around the star. Around white dwarfs these rings sit very close in, about the size of the Sun, and the rubble slowly rains down onto the star. Some discs show only dust, which glows in infrared. A few also contain gas, which leaves sharp emission lines in the spectrum.

Analogy: Like a ring of crumbs left around a plate after someone broke a cookie over it.

Why astronomers care

  • It is the leftover evidence that planets once existed around the star.
  • Its composition tells you what the destroyed bodies were made of.
