Galactic tide

The stretching pull the whole Milky Way exerts on a system that is spread out over a large distance.

Plain explanation

Gravity from the Galaxy's mass is slightly different on one side of a wide system than on the other. That difference is a tide, the same idea as the Moon raising tides on Earth.

For tight systems like planets around a star it's negligible, but for wide binaries it slowly twists the orbit, swinging its eccentricity and tilt up and down over long cycles.

Analogy: Like ocean tides, but caused by the whole Galaxy instead of the Moon, and acting over millions of years.

Why astronomers care

  • It shapes wide binaries and the distant Oort cloud of comets.
  • It can indirectly push planets onto extreme orbits.
