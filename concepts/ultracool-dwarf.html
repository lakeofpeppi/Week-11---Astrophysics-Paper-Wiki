Ultracool dwarf

The coolest stars and brown dwarfs, spectral type M7 and later (L, T, Y), colder than about 2500 K.

Plain explanation

They bridge the gap between the smallest real stars and giant planets. Many are brown dwarfs, “failed stars” too light to keep fusing hydrogen.

They're cool enough to have clouds, dust and molecules in their atmospheres, and they shine mostly in the infrared.

Analogy: Embers left after a fire: no longer flaming, but still glowing warm.

Why astronomers care

  • They are lookalikes of giant planets that are much easier to observe.
  • Some show auroras and radio bursts, like Jupiter.
