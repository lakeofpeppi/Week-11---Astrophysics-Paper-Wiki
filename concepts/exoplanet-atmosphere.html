Exoplanet atmosphere

The gas layer around a planet outside our Solar System, if it has one.

Plain explanation

An atmosphere changes how heat moves around a planet and leaves fingerprints in its spectrum (e.g. CO₂ absorbing certain wavelengths).

No atmosphere means the dayside stays very hot (the “bare rock” limit). A thick one spreads heat to the night side.

Analogy: A planet's blanket: without it, the sunny side bakes and the night side freezes.

Why astronomers care

  • An atmosphere is needed for liquid water and possibly life.
  • It tells us how a planet formed and evolved.
