The Moon

Earth's natural satellite. In this wiki, it's both a light source and a place to build a detector.

Plain explanation

The Moon has no light of its own; it reflects sunlight, and how much depends on its phase (full vs. new), on a 29.5-day cycle.

Because it has no atmosphere and is far from Earth's human and natural noise, it's also attractive as a site for very sensitive instruments.

Analogy: A giant streetlight in the sky that dims and brightens over a month, and also a quiet library far from the city.

Why astronomers care

  • Moonlight brightens the night sky and limits how faint astronomers can see.
  • Several proposed observatories would sit on the lunar surface.
