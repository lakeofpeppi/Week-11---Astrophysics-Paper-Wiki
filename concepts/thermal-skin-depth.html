Thermal skin depth

How deep a temperature change reaches into a surface during one heating and cooling cycle.

Plain explanation

δ = √(kP / (ρcpπ)), where P is the length of the cycle (Eq. 2 in the paper). Longer cycles push heat deeper.

So different observations see different depths: an eclipse (a few hours) only probes millimetres, a day-night cycle reaches centimetres, and seasons reach metres.

Analogy: A quick hot shower only warms your skin; a long sauna warms you much deeper.

Why astronomers care

  • It explains why two instruments can measure different thermal inertia for the same moon.
  • Comparing depths reveals layering: loose powder on top, more compact material below.
