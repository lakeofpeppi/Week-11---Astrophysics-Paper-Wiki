Orbital eccentricity

How stretched an orbit is, from 0 (a perfect circle) to almost 1 (a very long, thin oval).

Plain explanation

Written as e. Earth's orbit is nearly circular (e ≈ 0.017). At high e, a planet swings very close to its star and then far away again.

Eccentricity can be pumped up by gravitational nudges from other bodies, so it acts like a fossil record of a system's past disturbances.

Analogy: A circle drawn carefully (e = 0) versus one you've squashed into a skinny oval (e close to 1).

Why astronomers care

  • It reveals a system's dynamical history.
  • Very high eccentricity can send a planet close enough to its star to be changed by tides.
