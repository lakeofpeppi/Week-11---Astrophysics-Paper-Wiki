Regolith

The loose layer of dust, grains or broken material covering a solid surface.

Plain explanation

Regolith forms when a surface is broken up over time, for example by tiny meteorite impacts, or when material falls and settles on it. On the Moon it's fine rock dust; on icy moons it's grains of ice.

How tightly the grains are packed and how big they are decides how the surface handles heat and light, so regolith structure shapes almost every remote measurement.

Analogy: Fresh powdery snow lying on top of a frozen, packed driveway.

Why astronomers care

  • Landers need to know whether they'll land on hard ground or deep powder.
  • Misunderstanding the regolith leads to wrong readings of temperature and spectra.
