Thermal inertia

How strongly a surface resists changing its temperature.

Plain explanation

Defined as Γ = √(kρcp): it combines thermal conductivity k, density ρ and heat capacity cp (Eq. 1 in the paper). High Γ means temperature changes slowly; low Γ means it heats and cools quickly.

It can't be measured directly. Scientists watch how fast a surface cools (during an eclipse or at night) and fit a model to find Γ.

Analogy: At the beach at night, the sand gets cold fast (low thermal inertia) while the sea stays warm (high thermal inertia).

Why astronomers care

  • It reveals what a surface is made of and how it's packed, from millions of kilometres away.
  • It's a key input for choosing safe landing sites.
