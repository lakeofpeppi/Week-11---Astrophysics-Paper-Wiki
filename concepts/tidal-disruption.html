Tidal disruption

When a body gets so close to something massive that gravity pulls it apart.

Plain explanation

Gravity pulls harder on the side of an object that is closer. Near a very dense object like a white dwarf, that difference is so big that it can stretch an asteroid until it breaks. Below a certain distance, a loose rubble pile falls apart easily, but a stronger or denser body (like solid iron) can survive a bit closer before breaking.

Analogy: Like pulling a piece of bread dough from both ends: if one end is pulled much harder, it tears apart.

Why astronomers care

  • It explains how planetary material ends up in discs around white dwarfs.
  • How close a body survives tells you how strong or dense it must be.
