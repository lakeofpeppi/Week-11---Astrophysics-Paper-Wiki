Rubin Observatory

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory: a large survey telescope in Chile that photographs the whole southern sky every few nights.

Plain explanation

Rubin uses an 8.4 m telescope and a huge camera to repeatedly scan the sky. Over ten years it builds a time-lapse movie of the universe (the LSST survey).

Because it's so sensitive and images such a wide area, satellite streaks are a big worry for its data.

Analogy: A security camera for the entire sky, so anything bright that flies past ends up on the footage.

Why astronomers care

  • Satellite streaks can ruin pixels or fake real signals.
  • It's a benchmark for asking whether a satellite is bright enough to matter for astronomy.
