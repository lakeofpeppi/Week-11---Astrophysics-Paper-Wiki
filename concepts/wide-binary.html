Wide binary

Two stars orbiting each other from very far apart, here 1,000 to 100,000 times the Earth–Sun distance.

Plain explanation

A binary is two stars bound by gravity. In a wide binary they're so far apart that the companion is only loosely held, so it takes thousands to millions of years to complete one orbit.

Because the bond is so weak, outside forces like the Galaxy's tide and passing stars can slowly change the orbit or break the pair apart (astronomers call this ionization).

Analogy: Two friends holding hands across a whole park: it doesn't take much of a push to separate them.

Why astronomers care

  • Many planet-hosting stars have a wide companion.
  • The companion can disturb the planets, even from very far away.
