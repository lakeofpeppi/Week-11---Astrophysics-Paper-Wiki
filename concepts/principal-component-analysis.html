Principal component analysis

A method that finds the few main patterns that explain most of the variation in a dataset.

Plain explanation

PCA finds new axes (components) along which the data varies the most, ordered by how much variance each explains. It's a standard dimensionality-reduction tool in ML.

For a time series of spectra, each component is a “shape” of change across wavelengths, so you can see if different parts of the spectrum vary for different reasons.

Analogy: Like finding that most of a song's loudness changes come from just the drums and the bass, out of dozens of instruments.

Why astronomers care

  • It separates different physical causes of variability.
  • It reduces noisy, high-dimensional data to a few interpretable signals.
