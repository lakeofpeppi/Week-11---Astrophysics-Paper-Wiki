Surface brightness

Brightness per patch of sky, measured in magnitudes per square arcsecond (mag/arcsec²).

Plain explanation

For things that are spread out, like galaxies, nebulae or a satellite's streak, total brightness isn't what matters for detection. What matters is how bright each small patch is compared with the sky background.

A neat property: for a resolved object, surface brightness doesn't change with distance. It gets smaller, but each patch stays equally bright.

Analogy: A TV screen looks just as bright from across the room. It looks smaller, but each part of it isn't dimmer.

Why astronomers care

  • It decides whether something faint and fuzzy can be pulled out of the sky background.
  • Detection limits for surveys are given in mag/arcsec².
