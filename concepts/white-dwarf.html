White dwarf

The hot, Earth-sized core left behind when a Sun-like star dies.

Plain explanation

When a star like the Sun runs out of fuel, it throws off its outer layers and leaves a small, very dense core. That core is a white dwarf: about the size of Earth but with roughly half the Sun's mass, so a spoonful would weigh tonnes. It no longer makes energy, it just slowly cools down. Its gravity is so strong that heavy elements sink out of sight quickly, so if you see metals like calcium in its atmosphere, something must have fallen onto it recently.

Analogy: An ember left after a campfire burns out: no more fuel, but still glowing and slowly cooling.

Why astronomers care

  • It is what the Sun will become, so white dwarfs show the future of our Solar System.
  • Metals on their surface reveal rocky planetary material that fell in, which lets astronomers measure what other planets are made of.
