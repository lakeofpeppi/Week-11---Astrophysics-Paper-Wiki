Fisher matrix

A quick way to forecast how precisely a future experiment could measure some parameters.

Plain explanation

You assume “true” parameter values and ask how sharply the likelihood would peak around them. The sharper the peak, the better the measurement.

The inverse of the Fisher matrix gives the expected error bars and correlations, which are usually drawn as 1σ and 2σ ellipses. Tilted ellipses mean two parameters are degenerate (hard to tell apart).

Analogy: It's basically the Hessian (curvature) of a loss function at its minimum. Steep curvature means that parameter is pinned down well, and flat curvature means it isn't.

Why astronomers care

  • It lets people evaluate a detector before it's built.
  • It's cheap to compute compared with a full simulation, but it assumes nice Gaussian behaviour.
