Infrared excess

More infrared light than a star alone should give off, a sign of warm dust nearby.

Plain explanation

A star's temperature predicts how much light it should give off at each wavelength. If you measure noticeably more infrared light than that, something else is glowing too, usually dust heated to a few hundred or thousand degrees. The size of the excess hints at how much dust there is and where it sits.

Analogy: Like feeling extra warmth near a heater that a thermometer reading of the room alone wouldn't explain: something nearby is warm.

Why astronomers care

  • It is the easiest way to find dust discs, even when you can't see them directly.
  • It is how many dusty white dwarfs are first discovered in surveys.
