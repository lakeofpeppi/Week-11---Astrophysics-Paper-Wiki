Icy moon

A moon whose surface is mostly water ice, like Europa, Ganymede and Callisto (Jupiter) or Enceladus (Saturn).

Plain explanation

Far from the Sun, water freezes as hard as rock, so many moons of Jupiter and Saturn are covered in a thick shell of ice. Their surfaces are very cold, from about 50 K to under 200 K.

Several of them are thought to hide liquid water oceans under the ice shell, which is why they're major targets for space missions. This is different from the Moon (Earth's), which is rocky.

Analogy: A frozen lake with water still underneath, and a layer of powdery snow on top.

Why astronomers care

  • A hidden ocean could make them places where life is possible.
  • Missions like JUICE and Europa Clipper are studying them, and ESA plans a lander for Enceladus.
