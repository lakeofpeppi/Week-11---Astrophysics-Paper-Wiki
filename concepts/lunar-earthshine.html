Lunar-Earthshine

Moonlight that bounces off Earth and lights up the Earth-facing side of a satellite.

Plain explanation

Moonlight hits Earth, and part of it reflects back up into space. A satellite overhead therefore gets light from two directions: straight from the Moon, and from below, off Earth.

Surfaces that face Earth (nadir-facing) mostly see this bounced light. Don't confuse it with ordinary earthshine, which is sunlight reflected by Earth onto the Moon's dark side.

Analogy: A lamp on the ceiling lights the top of a table, but light bouncing off a white floor is what lights the table's underside.

Why astronomers care

  • Ignoring it makes brightness models underestimate how bright satellites are at night.
  • It changes which parts of a satellite are visible.
