Secondary eclipse

When a planet passes behind its star, so its own light briefly disappears from the total.

Plain explanation

A transit is the planet passing in front of the star. A secondary eclipse is the opposite: the planet goes behind. The tiny drop in total light, measured in parts per million (ppm), is exactly the planet's own glow.

In the infrared that glow is mostly heat, so the eclipse depth tells you the planet's dayside temperature.

Analogy: Measuring how bright a candle is by watching a room's total light dip when someone briefly hides it behind a wall.

Why astronomers care

  • It measures a planet's temperature without imaging it.
  • A hot dayside means no atmosphere is moving heat around the planet.
