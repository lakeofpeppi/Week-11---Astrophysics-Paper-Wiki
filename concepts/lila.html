LILA

Lunar Interferometer for Laser Astronomy: a proposed laser detector on the Moon.

Plain explanation

A laser interferometer bounces lasers between mirrors far apart and detects incredibly small changes in their distance (strain). That's how LIGO detects gravitational waves on Earth.

Putting one on the Moon could reach much lower frequencies than Earth-based detectors, which are limited by noise from our planet.

Analogy: A super-precise laser ruler. If space itself stretches a tiny bit, the ruler notices.

Why astronomers care

  • It would open a frequency window that ground detectors can't reach.
  • It's one of several reasons scientists want to build observatories on the Moon.
