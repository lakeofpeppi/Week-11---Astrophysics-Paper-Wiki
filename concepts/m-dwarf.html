M dwarf

The smallest, coolest and most common type of hydrogen-burning star: a red dwarf.

Plain explanation

M dwarfs are much smaller and dimmer than the Sun, but they make up most of the stars in the Galaxy. They're often very active, with strong flares and high-energy radiation.

The spectral type runs M0 (warmest) to M9 (coolest). The coolest ones, M7 and later, count as ultracool dwarfs.

Analogy: A small campfire compared with the Sun's bonfire, but one that throws a lot of sparks.

Why astronomers care

  • Their small size makes planets around them easier to detect and study.
  • Their strong radiation may strip planet atmospheres.
