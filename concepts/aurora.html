Aurora

Light produced when charged particles crash into an atmosphere along magnetic field lines, like the northern lights.

Plain explanation

On Earth, particles from the Sun cause auroras. On Jupiter, and on some ultracool dwarfs, strong magnetic fields accelerate particles that rain down near the poles.

Besides glowing, auroras can heat the upper atmosphere and change its chemistry, and they often come with bright radio emission.

Analogy: The northern lights, but on a tiny star, powered by its own magnetic field instead of the Sun.

Why astronomers care

  • Auroras reveal an object's magnetic field.
  • They may be one cause of brightness variations in brown dwarfs.
