Low Earth orbit

Orbits close to Earth, below about 2,000 km. Home of the ISS and most satellite constellations.

Plain explanation

Satellites in low Earth orbit (LEO) circle Earth roughly every 90 minutes. The ISS flies at about 400 km.

From the ground they're usually only visible around twilight: the satellite is still in sunlight, but your sky is already dark. Tracking them is part of space domain awareness (SDA), knowing what's up there and where.

Analogy: The busy inner ring road around a city: lots of traffic, moving fast, close to home.

Why astronomers care

  • LEO is getting crowded (Starlink, OneWeb, BlueBird…), which raises collision and tracking concerns.
  • Bright satellites leave streaks across astronomy images.
