Hot Jupiter

A giant gas planet orbiting extremely close to its star, often in just a few days.

Plain explanation

Giant planets probably can't form that close to their star. One idea is that they form farther out, get pushed onto a very stretched orbit, and then the star's tides slowly shrink and circularize the orbit.

In that picture, anything that pumps up eccentricity can help make hot Jupiters.

Analogy: A planet that got kicked toward the fireplace and got stuck there.

Why astronomers care

  • They were the first type of exoplanet found around a Sun-like star.
  • How they form is still debated.
