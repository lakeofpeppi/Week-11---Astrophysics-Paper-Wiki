JWST Rocky Worlds DDT Observations of LTT 1445 A b and c in Secondary Eclipse Using MIRI/F1500W

Jegug Ih et al. · arXiv:2611.17300 ↗

observationalplanetary
TL;DRJWST measured the heat glowing from two rocky planets around a nearby red dwarf by watching them disappear behind the star. Planet b is a bare rock with no thick atmosphere.

Research question

Red dwarf (M dwarf) stars blast their planets with strong radiation that may strip away atmospheres. The JWST Rocky Worlds program asks which rocky planets keep an atmosphere. This paper tests the two rocky planets LTT 1445 A b and c, in the closest such system (6.9 pc).

Data & tools

  • JWST MIRI imaging at 15 µm (F1500W filter): two secondary eclipses of each planet over three visits (Abstract, Sec. 2)
  • Archival JWST and HST transits and the MIRI/LRS spectrum of planet b (Sec. 3.1)
  • 220 radial velocity measurements (Abstract, Sec. 3)
  • Three independent data reductions (Sec. 4.1–4.3)
  • Bayesian “grid-trieval” and forward atmosphere models (Sec. 6.2–6.3)

Method

  1. Observe each planet passing behind its star (secondary eclipse) at 15 µm with MIRI (Sec. 2).
  2. Reduce the data three independent ways and check the eclipses are real (Sec. 4).
  3. Jointly fit all JWST light curves and radial velocities to refine sizes, masses and orbits (Sec. 5).
  4. Turn the eclipse depth into a brightness temperature and compare with the bare-rock maximum (Sec. 6.1).
  5. Compare with bare-surface and atmosphere models to see which atmospheres are still allowed (Sec. 6.2–6.3).

Conclusions

  • Planet b has no substantial atmosphere and is best explained by dark bare rock.
  • Planet c is poorly constrained because its eclipse may be grazing, but Venus-like atmospheres are ruled out.
  • Planet b is the closest planet to the predicted “Cosmic Shoreline” observed in eclipse, so the true line can't sit much farther toward the airless side.
  • Remaining thin-atmosphere options for b will be tested by upcoming JWST transmission programs.

Limitations

  • Planet c may have a grazing eclipse and overlaps with b's events, so its depth is uncertain (Sec. 7.1).
  • One photometric band (15 µm) for planet c; no spectrum (Sec. 7.4).
  • Thin or transparent atmospheres (≲ 0.1 bar) can't be detected in eclipse (Sec. 8).
  • Brightness temperatures depend on the stellar model used (Sec. 6.1.1).

To verify

  • Planet c's 1σ temperature range (+109 / −79 K) is much narrower than its 95.5% range (+352 / −154 K) (Sec. 6.1.2). Quote the confidence level with the number.
  • Sec. 8 describes the bare-rock model with AB = 0.1, while Sec. 6.3 gives a best fit of 0.12 ± 0.09. They're consistent, but don't treat 0.1 as a measured value.
